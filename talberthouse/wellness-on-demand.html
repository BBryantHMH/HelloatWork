Clinician recommended

Books, apps & podcasts our psychologists recommend

Picks from the Hello Mental Health team, for your own well-being, to share with the people you serve, and for leaders.

BookFor everyone

The Illustrated Happiness Trap / The Happiness Trap

by Russ Harris

An introduction to Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), useful for clients and clinicians alike. The Illustrated Happiness Trap is shorter, more visual, and more accessible; the original goes deeper.

Recommended by Dr. Michelle Maegly

BookTo share with clients

A Better Share

by Morgan Cutlip, PhD

Dr. Morgan Cutlip helps couples view the mental load as a shared enemy to conquer versus a problem they have with each other, offering practical solutions for navigating the most common pain points couples struggle with.

Recommended by a Hello Mental Health client

BookTo share with clients

It's Not You

by Ramani Durvasula, PhD

Dr. Ramani Durvasula's foundational book on recognizing narcissistic abuse and recovering from it.

Recommended by Dr. Michelle Maegly

BookFor everyone

The Book of Joy

by Dalai Lama, Desmond Tutu, and Douglas Abrams

The Dalai Lama and Archbishop Desmond Tutu spend a week together discussing what actually makes a life joyful, even amid loss and hardship. A warm, conversational read on resilience from two people who have lived through real suffering.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Man's Search for Meaning

by Viktor E. Frankl

Psychiatrist Viktor Frankl draws on his experience surviving the Holocaust to argue that finding meaning, even in suffering, is what allows people to endure. A foundational text in existential psychology and still one of the most widely read books on purpose.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

The Power of Now

by Eckhart Tolle

Eckhart Tolle makes the case that most of our suffering comes from living in our heads rather than in the present moment. A gentle entry point into mindfulness for people who are not looking for anything clinical.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Four Thousand Weeks

by Oliver Burkeman

Oliver Burkeman reframes time management around a hard truth: the average human life is only about four thousand weeks long. It is less about productivity hacks and more about making peace with limits.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

H is for Hawk

by Helen Macdonald

Helen Macdonald processes grief after her father's sudden death by training a goshawk, one of the most difficult birds to tame. A beautifully written memoir on loss that does not try to wrap it in a bow.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

The Ten Thousand Doors of January

by Alix E. Harrow

A fantasy novel about a young woman who discovers hidden doors to other worlds and starts to question the life she has been told to accept. A good option for clients who connect with reflection through story rather than nonfiction.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor leaders

Impact with Love: Building Business for a Better World

by Greg Harmeyer

Cincinnati entrepreneur Greg Harmeyer writes about leading with love and purpose in business without losing sight of results. A useful read for practice owners or clients navigating leadership and burnout.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

The Untethered Soul: The Journey Beyond Yourself

by Michael A. Singer

Michael Singer explores what it looks like to stop identifying with every passing thought and feeling. A widely recommended guide to inner freedom for people newer to mindfulness work.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience

by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi

Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi explains the state of deep absorption people enter when fully engaged in a challenging, meaningful activity. Helpful for clients wanting to understand motivation and what makes an activity feel worthwhile.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Adult Children of Emotionally Immature Parents

by Lindsay C. Gibson, PsyD

Psychologist Lindsay Gibson explains how growing up with emotionally immature parents shapes adult relationships and self-worth, and offers a path toward healing. One of the most frequently recommended books for clients doing family-of-origin work.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Radical Compassion: Learning to Love Yourself and Your World with the Practice of RAIN

by Tara Brach

Tara Brach teaches RAIN (Recognize, Allow, Investigate, Nurture) as a practice for meeting difficult emotions with compassion instead of avoidance. A practical bridge between mindfulness and self-compassion work.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Attached: The New Science of Adult Attachment

by Amir Levine and Rachel Heller

Accessible attachment-style framework, very popular as a first read on relationship patterns.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Atomic Habits

by James Clear

Clear breaks down how small, consistent changes compound into meaningful behavior change, using frameworks like habit stacking and identity-based habits. A practical, non-clinical read that works well for clients who want structure around building or breaking a habit.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Burnout: The Secret to Unlocking the Stress Cycle

by Emily Nagoski, PhD, and Amelia Nagoski, DMA

Emily and Amelia Nagoski explain why stress builds up in the body and how to actually complete the stress cycle, not just manage it. Especially relevant for people in caring professions.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Trauma Stewardship

by Laura van Dernoot Lipsky with Connie Burk

A field guide for people who help others through hard things, on recognizing the toll of that work and caring for yourself so you can keep doing it.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

The Body Keeps the Score

by Bessel van der Kolk, MD

Bessel van der Kolk explains how trauma lives in the body, not just the mind, and describes body-based approaches to healing.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

BookFor everyone

Maybe You Should Talk to Someone

by Lori Gottlieb

Therapist Lori Gottlieb's warm, funny memoir of being a therapist who also goes to therapy. A gentle read on what therapy is really like.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

AppFor everyone

Headspace

A meditation and mindfulness app with guided sessions for stress, sleep, and focus, built for people who are brand new to meditation. Good for clients who want a low-pressure way to start a daily practice.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

AppFor everyone

Finch

A self-care app where users care for a virtual bird by completing small daily wellness goals, like drinking water or getting outside. A gentle, game-like option for clients who respond better to habit tracking than traditional journaling.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

AppFor everyone

Calm

A meditation and sleep app known for its Sleep Stories, breathing exercises, and daily content library, often seen as Headspace's closest competitor with a stronger emphasis on sleep.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

AppFor everyone

Insight Timer

A free meditation app with one of the largest libraries in the category, offering guided sessions from thousands of independent teachers plus live classes and courses.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

AppFor everyone

Sanvello

An app built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) with mood tracking, guided journeys, and a peer community. Many insurance plans cover the premium features.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant

PodcastFor everyone

Psych Waves

by Dr. Teresa Anderson

Cincinnati psychiatrist, host of a biweekly show covering trauma, depression, PTSD, and newer treatment options like Ketamine/Spravato, mixing solo episodes with guest interviews.

Recommended by Dr. Bailey Bryant
