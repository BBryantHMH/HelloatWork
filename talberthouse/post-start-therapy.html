How Do I Know When It's Time to Start Therapy?

Andrea Antczak · October 1, 2026 · Hello Mental Health

You’ve been wondering whether therapy might be right for you. Maybe things feel a little harder than usual, or you’ve been carrying the same worries for longer than you’d like. The question isn’t always easy to answer: When is the right time to reach out for help?

It’s one of the most common questions people ask before starting. Here are some signs that therapy might be a helpful next step.

Signs It Might Be Time to Consider Therapy

1. Your Feelings Are Sticking Around

Everyone has hard emotions. But when sadness, anxiety, anger, or emptiness lingers for weeks or months, or starts getting in the way of daily life, it may be time for support. Therapy can help you understand what’s going on and find ways to manage it.

2. You’re Leaning on Coping Habits That Don’t Help

When stress piles up, it’s common to reach for quick fixes like drinking more, eating to cope, pulling away from people, or avoiding responsibilities. If those patterns are turning into habits, a therapist can help you find ways of coping that work better over time.

3. Your Relationships Are Suffering

Trouble communicating, frequent conflict, or feeling disconnected from the people who matter to you can be a sign that something needs attention. A therapist can help you spot patterns and build stronger connections.

4. You’ve Been Through a Big Change

Job loss, divorce, a move, grief, and even happy changes like a new baby can take a lot out of you. Therapy gives you a place to process what happened and adjust.

5. You Feel Stuck

Sometimes there’s no crisis, just a steady sense that something is off. You might feel unmotivated, directionless, or like you’re going through the motions. Therapy can help you reconnect with what matters to you and figure out where to go from here.

6. Your Body Is Sending Signals

Headaches, stomach problems, fatigue, or sleep changes can sometimes be linked to stress or mental health. Start with your medical provider to check for physical causes. If those are ruled out, therapy can help with the stress and emotions that may be playing a part, since mind and body are closely connected.

7. You Want to Understand Yourself Better

You don’t need to be in crisis to get something out of therapy. Plenty of people go to learn more about themselves, grow, and live more intentionally.

What the Research Says

Therapy has a strong track record for many concerns, including anxiety, depression, trauma, and relationship problems. The American Psychiatric Association notes that about 75 percent of people who enter psychotherapy show some benefit from it.

You Don’t Have to Wait Until Things Get Worse

You don’t need to hit a breaking point to reach out. Getting support earlier can make things easier to work through before they become harder to manage.

You wouldn’t wait until a cough turned serious to see a doctor. Emotional health works the same way.

What to Expect When You Start

If you’ve never been to therapy, the unknown can be part of what holds you back. Here’s what the beginning usually looks like:

Finding a good fit. The relationship with your therapist matters a lot. It’s okay to ask about their approach, and it’s okay to try someone else if it doesn’t feel right.

The first session. Your therapist will explain how therapy works, answer your questions, ask about what brought you in, and help you start naming your goals. You share at your own pace.

Working together. Over time, you and your therapist decide what to focus on and adjust as you go.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a serious problem to start therapy?

No. People start therapy for all kinds of reasons, from ongoing stress to life changes to simply wanting to understand themselves better.

How do I find a therapist?

Look for a licensed provider whose approach and availability fit your needs. You can find a provider through the library, or ask your doctor for a referral.

What if I need help right now?

If you’re in crisis, call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time.

Taking the First Step

Deciding to start therapy takes courage. It’s a way of recognizing that you deserve support. If you’ve been wondering whether it might be right for you, that wondering is worth listening to.
